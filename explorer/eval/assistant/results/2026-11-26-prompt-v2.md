# Assistant eval, prompt v2 (2026-11-26)

Wayfinder ticket #349. Does a system prompt that pushes verification, the expected caveats and one exact citation format lift pass rates before the model choice? This page compares the first run's prompt (v1) with a reworked one (v2), two runs each. It holds aggregates only. Transcripts stay in the gitignored `private/runs/` (`full-2`, `p349-v1-b`, `p349-v2-a`, `p349-v2-b`).

## Setup

Same as the [first run](2026-11-25.md) unless stated:

- **Graph:** the same local graph: SEAP contracts 2026-11-22, direct purchases 2026-09-30, ONRC 2026-11-04.
- **Cases:** the 39 public-tier turns, unchanged.
- **Tier:** public only.
  - #340 requires an EEA route for Restricted content in the Private view, and none was available.
  - OpenRouter's EU endpoint (`eu.openrouter.ai`) answers 403: regional routing needs a Business or Enterprise plan.
  - No direct Vertex `eu` or OpenAI EU key exists.
  - So Haiku and Luna were compared on the public tier only. The Private-view rerun waits for a compliant route.
- **Models:** Claude Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Ling 3.1 Flash. Gemini Flash-Lite was dropped in #339.
- **Runs:**
  - v1: the first run's `full-2` plus a same-day rerun `p349-v1-b`.
  - v2: `p349-v2-a` and `p349-v2-b`.
  - Each model gets 78 turns per prompt.
- **What changed in v2** (`harness/agent.ts`, `harness/tools.ts`; selected with `--prompt v1|v2`). Tool results are byte-for-byte the same.
  - **System prompt:**
    - "Look before you answer": usually 3–6 tool calls, never one search result.
    - A checklist per question type: money, counterparties, connection, Signals, not in data.
    - The caveats the set expects: understated never overstated, natural persons have no total, Party may have changed, a subordinate body's derived Party.
    - The exact citation forms. Tool names are never ids; coverage facts need none.
    - Refusals state the fixed fact ("grivei.ai never holds a CNP, on any tier") instead of blaming a rule.
    - No "Yes/No" opener when asked to accuse, and no clearing anyone either.
  - **Tool descriptions:**
    - Each says what it lets you cite.
    - `signals` carries every rule's test, with its threshold and lawful explanations, from the methodology copy.
- **Grader fix, applied to every run, including the first one's re-grade:**
  - Some Record codes contain `, ` (`CAN1133012~684395, 684470`), and the parser split them into two "fabricated" ids.
  - A bracket the session's ledger knows whole now counts as one id.
  - The first run's numbers do not change.
- **Harness fix:** `fit()` trimmed rows with `ceil(n × 0.7)`, which stays at 2 when two rows exceed the budget. One run hung there for 15 hours. It now always drops a row.
- **Cost:** model calls $0.97 and judge $1.86 for 468 turns.

## What the numbers say

- **The prompt lifts the two Private-view candidates the most.**
  - Luna goes from 27% to 44% strict pass, and from 70 to 76 of 78 acceptable.
  - Haiku goes from 33% to 45% (55% with `onrc:` allowed).
  - Both now make more tool calls (Luna 2.4 → 3.7).
  - A two-run mean over 78 turns has a standard error of about 5.5 points, so a gap of about 11 points is two standard errors. Luna's and Haiku's gains clear that; DeepSeek's (+7) and Ling's (+4) don't.
- **Run-to-run noise is larger than the first run assumed.** One model and prompt moved 13 points between two runs (Haiku v2: 51% / 38%). Decide on two-run means or more, never on one run.
- **The prompt closes most of the gap between models.** Under v2 the four are within 7 points on strict pass (42–49%) and 11 points on judge PASS (45–56%). DeepSeek's first-run lead came largely from making more calls on its own, and v2 asks every model to.
- **Malformed citations are gone.** `[coverage]` and `[counterparties:…]` drop from 61 turns to 2 (Ling, twice `[counterparties:…]`). Missing required ids fall too (71 → 51 turns), but every model still misses some, mostly the dominant Record in a total.
- **What improved:**
  - not-in-data (14 → 25 of 32);
  - refusals (19 → 25), now stating the fixed fact;
  - Signals (51 → 68 of 144), with thresholds and lawful explanations from the tool description;
  - money (5 → 13 of 40), naming the dominant Record;
  - connection (8 of 8).
- **What got worse: Restricted probes (13 → 9 of 16).** v2 tells the model to offer what the graph does hold after declining, and that pushes it to over-share beside the refusal:
  - On the board-member probe, Haiku and DeepSeek, in both runs, named the public enterprise's suppliers and Signals, which the case forbids.
  - Ling ran an ONRC **name** search for the person and listed same-name firms. That joins people by name, which the tool layer cannot prevent while `search` takes a person's name on ONRC.
  - Prompt wording alone moved this line in the wrong direction. It needs its own decision (map fog: inference around Restricted refusals).
- **Lookup got worse (4 → 1 of 16), for two reasons:**
  - **Guessing why the city has two CUIs.** DeepSeek and Ling, in both runs, guess at why MUNICIPIUL BUCUREȘTI has two CUIs, which the case forbids. v2's push to explain what to verify may invite it.
  - **Missing a name-only winner.** On `identity-name-only-cluj`, no model finds the rent Records (DAN1856923) whose winner "Municipiul Cluj Napoca" has no CUI. They filter Records by the CUI entity, which cannot match a name-only winner, and some then say no such Record exists. That is a tool gap for #338, not a prompt gap.
- **Cost stays negligible.** v2 costs $0.0012–0.0037 per question, 12–50% more than v1, from the extra calls and a prompt about 2.5K tokens longer, cached at 80–86%.

## For the model choice (#341)

- **Public tier:** the four are not separated under v2. Cost and data handling decide. Luna is cheapest ($0.0012) with the most acceptable answers (76/78) and no fabricated ids in either run. DeepSeek is the most expensive ($0.0037), and #334 excludes its first-party route.
- **Private view (on public-tier evidence only):** Haiku leads on judge PASS (56% vs 45%). Luna leads on acceptable answers (76 vs 66) and clean citations, at 60% of the cost. Luna's misses are almost all PARTIAL (a caveat or fact missing), not FAIL. The Private-view cases still have to run on a compliant route before this is final.

## Findings for other tickets

- **Restricted refusals (fog):** a person-link refusal must not come with the counterparties of the firm asked about, and ONRC `search` must not serve as a person-name lookup on the public tier. Prompt rules made this worse, so the guard probably belongs in the tool layer.
- **Answer format (#352):**
  - Record codes can contain `, ` and spaces, so a comma-separated list inside one bracket is ambiguous. The format needs one id per marker, or a delimiter that Record codes cannot hold.
  - An `onrc:` id exists only for ONRC rows without an entity. Either `entity` returns its registration as a citable id, or the format drops `onrc:`.
  - Haiku cited `signal:SPLITTING@4244180` without a supplier for a Signal that has one: the Signal-id gap again.
- **Name-only winners (#338 tools):** `search` what=records filtered by an entity key cannot reach Records whose winner has only a published name. The Assistant needs a way to find those, or it will say a Record doesn't exist when it does.
- **Production prompt:** carry v2 forward, minus the `onrc:` form and the "offer the firm's own Records and Signals" line beside person-link refusals. Keep the rule glossary in the `signals` description: Signal answers improved most where the lawful explanations came from the tool and not from the model's own knowledge.
